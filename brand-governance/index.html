I collected campaigns from 2017 to 2022, a period “marked by the mainstreaming of sociopolitical themes in brand campaigns” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 9). The period begins with the 2017 US Super Bowl, when “several ads broke with brand neutrality”, and ends with “the onset of the war in Ukraine” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 9). I conducted interviews from 2020 to 2022 for a broader project, and although I do not analyse them in the article, they “guided the identification of campaigns that were most publicly debated” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 9).

I selected campaigns “based on a logic of discursive dominance rather than simple commercial success”, with “substantial circulation in advertising trade press” and “engagement metrics” as criteria (Scalvini, 2026, p. 9). The corpus consists of “video advertisements circulated on digital platforms (YouTube, Instagram, Twitter) and accompanying digital visuals” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 9). I therefore prioritised “successful” instances of governance over “failed” or marginal attempts, since the aim of the analysis is “to map the normative centre of brand governance” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 9). I reviewed TikTok during data collection, but it “yielded limited campaign material within the period” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 9). The corpus is “heavily weighted toward Global North ‘super-brands’”, and I treat this focus as “a methodological necessity” rather than “a mere limitation” (Scalvini, 2026, pp. 9 to 11). To critique the regulatory mechanism, I chose to “target the centres at which these norms are produced rather than cataloguing the diversity of those excluded from them” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 11).

Approach

I read the campaigns through “a critical sociosemiotic approach” (Hodge and Kress, 1988), and I foreground “context-specific processes of meaning-making” rather than applying “a structuralist semiotics of fixed codes” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 12). With the resources listed in the table below, I examine how campaigns “stage ethical dilemmas, attribute moral authority, and position viewers within affectively charged frameworks” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 12).

Grounded coding

I developed the typology of strategies “through grounded coding” (Charmaz, 2006), in the stages that I describe in the article and quote in the table below (Scalvini, 2026, p. 12).

Four questions

I turned the concepts of the framework into four questions, which “linked micro-level features to broader concerns with moral positioning” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 12). Each question operationalises one concept, and I set out each question beside its concept in the table below, using the words of the article.

Validity

I claim “interpretive validity, grounded in coding transparency, conceptual saturation, and theoretical alignment” rather than “positivist reliability” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 12). Because the analysis is “production-oriented”, I examine “how campaigns construct moral discourse rather than how audiences receive or contest it”, and I identify reception studies as “a priority for future research” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 12).

    In the conclusion, I restate the central finding of the article, that “visibility expanded while the conditions of legibility did not” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 23). I set out two contributions, the distinction between regulatory and political recognition and the concept of brand governance as distinct from promotional culture and platform governance. I state that the focus on Global North super-brands, which I chose deliberately, “necessarily limits the generalisability of the findings” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 24). I then propose four directions for future research, namely non-Western publics, consumer activism, reception studies and longitudinal research on the DEI retrenchment.
