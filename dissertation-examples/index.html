Dissertation Examples

Curated by Dr Marco Scalvini, Senior Lecturer, London College of Communication (UAL), and Visiting Senior Fellow, Department of Media and Communications, LSE.

Thirty-one master's dissertations from the Media@LSE MSc Dissertation Series, which publishes some of the best dissertations from LSE's Department of Media and Communications. They were chosen as clear examples of theory and method for undergraduate and master's students. Each entry explains the theory the dissertation uses and how its discussion interprets the findings, and it links to the original PDF.

How to use them

  1. QuestionRead the abstract and introduction to see how a broad interest becomes a focused research question.
  2. FrameworkRead the theory chapter to see how the author moves from the literature to a few key ideas that the analysis then uses. Each entry below has a guide to its framework.
  3. MethodRead the methodology to see how the author chooses what to analyse, explains that choice, and carries out the analysis step by step.
  4. FindingsLook at how the findings are presented, with tables, annotated extracts and images that show the evidence.
  5. DiscussionRead the discussion to see how the author returns to the framework to interpret the findings and answer the research question. Each entry below has a guide to its discussion.

These are master's dissertations, so undergraduate projects will usually be shorter and narrower. Use them as models of structure, method and argument rather than templates. Do not cite them in your own work. Read and cite the published books and articles they draw on instead, such as the key readings listed in each guide.
