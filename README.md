## Dissertation Examples

The page helps students learn from published master's dissertations in media and communication. It presents thirty-one dissertations from the Media@LSE MSc Dissertation Series, chosen as clear examples of theory and method for undergraduate and master's students.

- For each dissertation, a short summary, the method and a plain-language guide to its theoretical framework, with key concepts, key readings, where to find the theory chapter and what to learn from it.
- For each dissertation, a guide to its discussion, which explains how the author returns to the framework to interpret the findings and answer the research question. The guide gives the PDF pages of the discussion and of the conclusion, with the headings of the discussion as they are printed.
- For each discussion, a table that gives the PDF pages of six moves, from the answer to the research question to the statement of limits and future research. Students can use the same six moves to check their own discussion.
- Search by title, author, theorist or idea, and filters by method and theme. A third filter separates the dissertations that discuss their findings in the findings chapter from those with a separate discussion chapter.

The dissertations are published by the Department of Media and Communications at the London School of Economics and Political Science in the [Media@LSE MSc Dissertation Series](https://www.lse.ac.uk/media-and-communications/research/msc-dissertation-series). They remain the work and copyright of their authors. This repository does not host the PDFs, and the page links to the originals on the LSE website.

The summaries and framework guides are short introductions based on each dissertation's abstract and theory chapter, whereas the discussion guides were written from the discussion and the conclusion of each dissertation. Both were prepared with AI assistance and checked against the original dissertations, and every quotation in the discussion guides was compared word for word with the text of the PDF page that it cites. Students should read each dissertation itself for the full argument, since the guides are short introductions to it.

## Brand Governance

The page accompanies the article "Brand governance in the platform economy: From political to regulatory recognition" (Scalvini, 2026), published in *New Media & Society* (https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448261475463), for the students of the Research Project who use semiotic analysis. It presents the corpus of 21 advocacy campaigns from 2017 to 2022 and the sociosemiotic method of the article. It also sets out the moves of the discussion of the article, and it ends with an exercise on the seven campaigns that the article does not classify.

| Group | Tabs |
|---|---|
| Corpus | Ledger, Corpus construction |
| Analysis | Method, Codes, Strategies, Matrix, Discussion, Terms |
| Seminar | Exercise |

The tables and the quotations come from the published version of the article, with its page and table numbers, and each quotation was checked word for word against that version. The links were checked on 7 October 2026, and the worksheet runs in the browser without saving any answer.

## Thesis Writing

The page guides students who write a BA or master's thesis in media and communication through the writing of the thesis, beginning with the introduction. It sets out the introduction in seven steps, from the motivation for the topic to the structure of the thesis, following the curator's guidelines for the thesis.

- For each step, the guidance, a passage from a worked example of an imagined thesis and an example from the introduction of "Brand governance in the platform economy" (Scalvini, 2026), with its page number.
- For each step, two phrases that students can add to their draft and adapt, adapted from the [Academic Phrasebank](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/) of the University of Manchester, with a link to the matching heading of the Phrasebank.
- A draft box for each step, and a section that joins the drafts into one introduction, which students can copy or download as a text file. The page does not save the drafts.
- The worked example joined into one introduction of four paragraphs, with its references.

The quotations in the worked example and in the published examples were checked word for word against the published versions of their sources, and the links were checked on 9 October 2026. The worked example was written for the page, so students should not cite it.
