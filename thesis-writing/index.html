Thesis Writing

I prepared this page for students who write a BA or master’s thesis in media and communication. On this page you can plan the introduction in seven steps, each with guidance, a worked example and a published example, phrases that you can adapt and a box for your own draft. I will add the other chapters of the thesis in the same format.

The guidance follows my guidelines for the introduction of a thesis in media and communication. The published examples come from the introduction of my article “Brand governance in the platform economy” (Scalvini, 2026), with page numbers from the version published in New Media & Society. doi.org/10.1177/14614448261475463

The introduction in seven steps

In the introduction, you define your research topic and explain its significance for media studies, in particular for the study of advertising and promotional communication. You also place your research within the existing literature and identify the gap that it addresses, which is where you establish the originality of your project. The seven steps follow the outline in my guidelines, from the motivation for the topic to the structure of the thesis.

About the worked example

In the worked example, I follow one imagined thesis through the seven steps, so that you can see how the steps connect. In the thesis, a student analyses the posts that four beauty brands published on Instagram for International Women’s Day in 2022 and in 2025, before and after the withdrawal of global brands from DEI initiatives. I wrote the example for this page, so do not cite it, although its references are real and I checked each quotation against its source.

Using the phrases

Each step offers two phrases that you can add to your draft and adapt, by replacing the words in square brackets with the terms of your own project. The phrases are adapted from the Academic Phrasebank of the University of Manchester, which offers more phrases for each step.

Following a survey of academics by Davis and Morley (2013), the Phrasebank reports that a reused phrase is acceptable when it has no original construction and does not express the point of view of another writer. Acceptability also declines beyond about nine words, and the phrase should contain no more than four generic content words.

  1. 01

    MotivationWhy is the topic important? So What test

    Start by explaining why you chose the topic and why this area of advertising or promotional communication needs further research. Show its relevance to media studies, and in particular to the ways in which advertising shapes cultural and ethical norms. For instance, you might examine how brands engage with social movements such as Black Lives Matter, or how they commodify activism. This is the So What test, which asks why the topic is important today.

    Worked example

    Several global brands have withdrawn from initiatives such as Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) in recent years, in what Scalvini (2026, pp. 2, 23) calls the “post-2022 DEI retrenchment”. These withdrawals concern advertising directly, since marketing “simultaneously enables the visibility and erasure” of feminist and Black social justice activist issues (Sobande, 2020, p. 2723).

    Example from a published article
    “Recent corporate withdrawals from initiatives such as Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) are frequently analysed as lapses in corporate commitment or departures from stated values (Rim et al., 2020; Villagra et al., 2021).” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 2)

    I open with a recent development, the withdrawal of corporations from DEI initiatives, and with the way in which earlier studies interpret it.

    Phrases you can adapt

    • Since [year], [brands] have [adopted X], and [audiences or activists] have questioned whether [this practice] [does Z].

    • In [year], [group] responded to [brand]’s [campaign] by [action], and since then [journalists or regulators] have treated X as a public question.

    Phrases adapted from the Academic Phrasebank, University of Manchester, where you can find more for this step.

  2. 02

    Thesis questionWhat is your central research question?

    State the central research question that guides your investigation. The question should reflect an interpretative approach, which calls for a detailed analysis rather than a simple answer. One question of this kind is “In what ways do promotional communications exploit performative activism to engage digital audiences?”

    Worked example

    In this thesis, I ask how four beauty brands represented women in the posts that they published on Instagram for International Women’s Day in 2022 and in 2025. I examine which women appear in these posts before and after the retrenchment, and on what terms they appear.

    Example from a published article
    “From this perspective, the central question is not whether advocacy serves brand positioning, but how it structures the moral legibility of claims within platformed public spheres.” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 3)

    I state the central question in one sentence, and I then divide it into two research questions.

    Phrases you can adapt

    • In this thesis, I ask how [brands] [do X] in [context], and with what consequences for [group].

    • I examine in what ways [organisations] [use X] to [achieve Y].

    Phrases adapted from the Academic Phrasebank, University of Manchester, where you can find more for this step.

  3. 03

    Literature reviewWhat has been established?

    Write a brief review of the research on your topic, and summarise the key findings and debates in media studies on advertising and on your specific area, such as brand activism or ethical branding. With the review, you place your research within the field and acknowledge what studies have already established, and you show that you know the current debates in media studies. For instance, you could refer to studies that criticise the role of brands in movements such as Blackout Tuesday, or to studies of performative activism and of the ethics of visibility in advertising. You can find sources in the List of Journals.

    Worked example

    Studies of brand activism have asked whether campaigns are authentic, and Vredenburg et al. (2020, p. 444) describe authentic brand activism as the match of “activist messaging, purpose, and values with prosocial corporate practice”. In a critical study of “femvertising”, Sobande (2020, p. 2723) identifies the “racialised and gendered subject positions” that brands use in this advertising.

    Example from a published article
    “Research remains organised around a functionalist logic that divides into intent-oriented interrogations of authenticity and “woke-washing” (Becker-Olsen et al., 2006; Vredenburg et al., 2020) and impact-oriented assessments of reputational and stakeholder outcomes (Kim and Meganck, 2025; Wagner et al., 2020).” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 3)

    I group the studies into two strands, so that the reader sees how the research is divided before I state the gap.

    Phrases you can adapt

    • Studies of X have examined how [organisations] [do Y], with particular attention to [Z] (Author, year, p. #).

    • Studies of X agree that [A], but they disagree on whether [B] (Author, year, p. #; Author, year, p. #).

    Phrases adapted from the Academic Phrasebank, University of Manchester, where you can find more for this step.

  4. 04

    Gap in the literatureWhat questions remain?

    After the review, identify the gap that your research will address, namely the questions that remain unanswered and the aspects of advertising and promotional communication that studies have not examined. At this point you establish the originality of your project. For instance, studies of brand activism may not have examined how these campaigns affect the formation of consumer identity on digital platforms, or how audiences in different regions receive the ethical messages of global brands.

    Worked example

    A significant gap remains, however, in the study of femvertising after the retrenchment, since both studies were published in 2020 and analyse earlier campaigns. In the conclusion of his article, Scalvini (2026, p. 24) calls for longitudinal research “tracking the consequences of the post-2022 DEI retrenchment”.

    Example from a published article
    “A significant gap remains, however. Scholarship has yet to articulate how this normative power interacts with the affordances of networked publics (Boyd, 2010; Davis and Chouinard, 2016) to produce specific regimes of visibility (Bucher, 2018).” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 3)

    I state that a gap remains, and in the next sentence I specify what earlier studies have not yet explained.

    Phrases you can adapt

    • Studies of X analyse [campaigns or texts], whereas few studies examine how [audiences] [respond to them].

    • A significant gap remains, however, in the study of how [organisations] [do Y] in [context].

    Phrases adapted from the Academic Phrasebank, University of Manchester, where you can find more for this step.

  5. 05

    Relevance and originalityHow does your research contribute?

    Explain why your research is relevant and why it is necessary now. State what your project contributes to the understanding of contemporary advertising and promotional communication, since this is where you demonstrate its originality. For instance, your study may provide new knowledge about the relation between promotional communication and social justice, or a critical analysis of how global brands construct and commodify ethical narratives.

    Worked example

    The originality of the thesis is that I compare the posts of the same brands before and after the retrenchment, so that any change in the representation of women becomes observable. The comparison is also relevant to advertising practice, since practitioners can use it to see whether the withdrawal from DEI changes the campaigns that they produce.

    Example from a published article
    “The primary theoretical contribution of this article is the distinction between political recognition and regulatory recognition as modes through which brand governance operates within the platform economy.” (Scalvini, 2026, pp. 3 to 4)

    I name the contribution as a distinction between two concepts, which I develop in the theoretical framework.

    Phrases you can adapt

    • This thesis is relevant now because [organisations or audiences] have [changed practice] since [event or year].

    • The originality of the thesis is that I apply [concept] to [case or corpus], which earlier studies of X have not examined.

    Phrases adapted from the Academic Phrasebank, University of Manchester, where you can find more for this step.

  6. 06

    ObjectiveWhat are you trying to achieve?

    Define the objective of your thesis, which may be to examine a phenomenon or a theoretical concept, or to analyse advertising strategies critically. For example, you might aim to examine how brands put selective empathy into practice in their messages. You might also aim to criticise the ways in which brands use social justice movements for promotional gain, without a substantial commitment to the causes that they support.

    Worked example

    My objective is to extend the distinction between “political recognition and regulatory recognition” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 3) to femvertising after the retrenchment. I therefore examine whether the brands address women as “political claimants” or as “objects of empathy” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 23).

    Example from a published article
    “Rather than asking whether campaigns express authenticity or achieve impact, the critical task is to examine how brand governance defines the conditions of visibility (Brighenti, 2007), determining which grievances can appear, on what terms, and within which grammars of legitimacy.” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 3)

    I state the objective in contrast with the question that earlier studies ask, within a single sentence.

    Phrases you can adapt

    • My objective is to analyse how [brands] [do Y], and to extend [concept] to [context].

    • I also aim to explain why [organisations] [do Y], and to assess the consequences for [group].

    Phrases adapted from the Academic Phrasebank, University of Manchester, where you can find more for this step.

  7. 07

    ExpectationsWhat will the thesis cover?

    Close the introduction with a brief outline of the rest of the thesis. Explain how you structure the thesis and how each chapter contributes to the answer to your central research question. You can mention your theoretical framework and your method, such as discourse analysis or thematic analysis, and give a brief overview of your case studies or data sources, such as social media campaigns or advertisements.

    Worked example

    In Chapter 2, I set out the studies of femvertising and the theory of recognition. In Chapter 3, I explain how I selected the posts and how I apply semiotic analysis to their images and captions. In Chapter 4, I present and discuss the findings, and in Chapter 5, I state my conclusions and the limits of the thesis.

    Example from a published article
    “Drawing on a sociosemiotic analysis of global advocacy campaigns from 2017 to 2022, the study identifies three techno-normative strategies through which this governance operates and examines how they interact with platform affordances to produce regulatory rather than political recognition.” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 4)

    An article has no chapters, so I close the introduction with the method and the findings. In a thesis, you also give the order of the chapters.

    Phrases you can adapt

    • I analyse [number] [campaigns or posts] from [period] through [method].

    • In Chapter 2, I set out the theoretical framework and in Chapter 3 the method, before presenting and discussing the findings in Chapter 4 and concluding in Chapter 5.

    Phrases adapted from the Academic Phrasebank, University of Manchester, where you can find more for this step.

The worked example as one introduction

Below, the seven passages of the worked example appear as four paragraphs, in the form that they would take in the introduction of the thesis.

Introduction

Several global brands have withdrawn from initiatives such as Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) in recent years, in what Scalvini (2026, pp. 2, 23) calls the “post-2022 DEI retrenchment”. These withdrawals concern advertising directly, since marketing “simultaneously enables the visibility and erasure” of feminist and Black social justice activist issues (Sobande, 2020, p. 2723). In this thesis, I ask how four beauty brands represented women in the posts that they published on Instagram for International Women’s Day in 2022 and in 2025. I examine which women appear in these posts before and after the retrenchment, and on what terms they appear.

Studies of brand activism have asked whether campaigns are authentic, and Vredenburg et al. (2020, p. 444) describe authentic brand activism as the match of “activist messaging, purpose, and values with prosocial corporate practice”. In a critical study of “femvertising”, Sobande (2020, p. 2723) identifies the “racialised and gendered subject positions” that brands use in this advertising. A significant gap remains, however, in the study of femvertising after the retrenchment, since both studies were published in 2020 and analyse earlier campaigns. In the conclusion of his article, Scalvini (2026, p. 24) calls for longitudinal research “tracking the consequences of the post-2022 DEI retrenchment”.

The originality of the thesis is that I compare the posts of the same brands before and after the retrenchment, so that any change in the representation of women becomes observable. The comparison is also relevant to advertising practice, since practitioners can use it to see whether the withdrawal from DEI changes the campaigns that they produce. My objective is to extend the distinction between “political recognition and regulatory recognition” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 3) to femvertising after the retrenchment. I therefore examine whether the brands address women as “political claimants” or as “objects of empathy” (Scalvini, 2026, p. 23).

In Chapter 2, I set out the studies of femvertising and the theory of recognition. In Chapter 3, I explain how I selected the posts and how I apply semiotic analysis to their images and captions. In Chapter 4, I present and discuss the findings, and in Chapter 5, I state my conclusions and the limits of the thesis.
